    How the logic works

    Expected recovery internally = balance still owed (principal + interest) × chance the user pays on their own (by days late; ×0.5 if they paid <10% of installments; ×0.75 with 2+ collection notes; ×1.5 if they paid in the last 30 days; ×0.5 if no payment in 90+ days).
    Extra signals change the chance they pay if we keep chasing: ▲ paid off loans before, pays on time, employer known, customer for 2+ years, paying more lately, paid recently · ▼ no phone, no or expired card, card declined, new customer (<6 months), pays late most of the time, paid <10% of installments, contacted 2+ times without payment, token payment, no payment in a long time.
    Expected recovery via the agency = balance still owed × 45% (what the agency is expected to collect) − Fl 250 agency fee. If the internal number is higher, we keep it internally.

    1. Keep internal – reminders 0–30 days late.
    2. Small balance less than Fl 300 still owed (principal + interest): hidden from the lists – not worth the agency. Keep reminding; write off from day 120.
    3. Keep internal – still paying late, but paid at least 20% of their past due amount in the last 30 days (a smaller token payment doesn't count).
    4. Keep internal – better internally when we expect to collect more by handling it ourselves than via the agency after its Fl 250 fee.
    5. Send to agency NOW 70+ days late and all criteria met.
    6. Final notice – agency at day 70 31–69 days late and the agency would come out ahead.
    Logic levers (change and everything recalculates)

    Balance owed = what is still unpaid on the open plans: principal balance + interest balance. Both the internal and the agency recovery are calculated on that full balance. Agency figures come from the Deurwaarder dashboard (only 45% of agency cases ever paid anything; first payment after ~13 months). Cure chances are estimates. The latest list is saved in Suave's Cloudflare database (password protected, the same login as Fraud Contacts); loading a new list deletes the old one.
